## Overview

This submodule addresses the conceptual, mathematical, and intuitive exploration of Classical Mechanics. The content spans from kinematics and Newton's Laws to structural statics, energy conservation, and rigid body rotational dynamics, using differential and integral calculus as the primary modeling tool.

## Module Topics

* **Kinematics (The Geometry of Motion)**
  * Vector tools: decomposition, dot product ($\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}$), and cross product ($\vec{a} \times \vec{b}$)
  * Differential kinematics in 1D, 2D, and 3D ($v = \frac{dx}{dt}$, $a = \frac{dv}{dt}$) and projectile motion
  * Angular kinematics, centripetal acceleration ($a_c$), and circular motion

* **Dynamics and Newton's Laws (The Causes of Motion)**
  * Newton's Laws and Force as the time rate of change of momentum ($\vec{F} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt}$)
  * Work as a line integral ($\int \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$), Work-Energy Theorem, and conservative systems
  * Center of mass, conservation of linear momentum, and variable-mass systems

* **Statics and Equilibrium (The Physics of Structures)**
  * First condition of equilibrium ($\sum \vec{F} = 0$) and concurrent forces
  * Torque concept ($\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F}$) and second condition of equilibrium ($\sum \vec{\tau} = 0$) in extended bodies

* **Advanced Rotations and Rigid Body Dynamics**
  * Moment of Inertia ($I$) and the fundamental torque relation ($\tau = I\alpha$)
  * Angular Momentum ($\vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{p}$) and its relation to torque ($\vec{\tau} = \frac{d\vec{L}}{dt}$)
  * Conservation of Angular Momentum, precession, and gyroscopic behavior

## Prerequisites

- **Differential and Integral Calculus I:** Derivative concepts as instantaneous rates and integrals as cumulative areas.
- **Analytic Geometry and Vectors:** Vector operations (dot and cross products) and Cartesian component decomposition.
- **Coordinate Systems:** Understanding polar and cylindrical coordinates for circular symmetry.
