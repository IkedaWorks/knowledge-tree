
# Physics III - Electromagnetism

Welcome to the Physics III study core. This space is dedicated to the conceptual, mathematical, and intuitive exploration of Electromagnetism — the fundamental force behind almost all modern technology, from electric motors to network signals and light.

The goal of these notes is to build a solid understanding of how electric and magnetic fields operate, focusing on developing geometric intuition to set up equations and understand the physical meaning behind each component.

---

## 🛠 Prerequisites and the Role of Calculus

Physics III uses Calculus as a practical modeling tool. We will not pursue pure mathematical derivations from scratch, but rather the ability to translate a physical problem into familiar mathematical operations:

*   **Calculus Tools (I, II, and III):** Understanding the concept of rates of change (derivatives) and the accumulation/summation of infinitesimal pieces (integrals). Knowing how to apply basic integration techniques to solve the modeled problems.

> [!NOTE]
>  
> When I first learned this subject, having a solid grasp of Calculus I and II helped a lot. However, you also need Calculus III to truly understand it rather than just memorizing equations. Therefore, the ideal approach is to study this module alongside Calculus III (Multivariable Calculus) or already have it mastered to truly comprehend this subject.

*   **Geometry and Vectors:** Vector decomposition, dot product (for flux and work), and cross product (essential in magnetism). Basic understanding of how cylindrical and spherical coordinates simplify our lives when dealing with spatial symmetries.
*   **Mechanics Concepts (Physics I):** Conservation of energy, work done by a force, and Newton's Laws applied to the motion of charges.

## 🗺 Learning Flow (Roadmap)

The content progresses from isolated static phenomena to the full unification of dynamic fields and waves:

### Block 1: Electrostatics (Static Electric Fields)
*   **Electric Charge and Coulomb's Law:** The behavior of charges (quantization and conservation) and the vector force between point charges.
*   **Electric Field and Continuous Distributions:** The concept of a field as a spatial disturbance. How to slice a charged object (rod, ring, disk) into infinitesimal pieces $dq$ and use integrals to sum the total field.
*   **Electric Flux and Gauss's Law:** The geometric interpretation of field lines crossing surfaces. Using Gaussian surfaces to find the field of symmetrical shapes in a much simpler way.

### Block 2: Potential, Energy, and Basic Circuits
*   **Electric Potential:** The concept of voltage ($V$) associated with work and the system's potential energy. The relationship between the electric field and potential difference.
*   **Capacitance and Dielectrics:** How to store energy in electric fields using capacitors, and how insulating materials (dielectrics) alter field behavior.

### Block 3: Magnetostatics (Static Magnetic Fields)
*   **The Magnetic Field ($\vec{B}$) and Magnetic Forces:** The origin of magnetism, the absence of magnetic monopoles, and how magnetic fields deflect moving charges and exert force on current-carrying wires.
*   **Sources of Magnetic Field (Biot-Savart & Ampère's Law):** How electric currents generate magnetic fields. Using the Biot-Savart Law to integrate wires and Ampère's Law as a symmetry tool (the magnetic equivalent to Gauss's Law).

### Block 4: Dynamic Electromagnetism and Waves
*   **Faraday's Law and Electromagnetic Induction:** The heart of motors and generators. How a time-varying magnetic field induces an electromotive force (voltage) and creates an electric field (Lenz's Law).
*   **Inductance and Maxwell's Equations:** The concept of inductors and RL/RLC circuits. Synthesizing the entire course into Maxwell's four equations, showing how varying electric and magnetic fields self-propagate.
*   **Electromagnetic Waves:** The logical conclusion of the course: light and radio waves viewed as electromagnetic disturbances traveling through space, carrying energy and momentum.
