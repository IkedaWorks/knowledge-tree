# Physics III - Electromagnetism

Welcome to the Physics III study core. This space is dedicated to the conceptual, mathematical, and intuitive exploration of Electromagnetism — the fundamental force behind almost all modern technology, from electric motors to network signals and light.

The goal of these notes is to build a solid understanding of how electric and magnetic fields operate, focusing on developing geometric intuition to set up equations and understand the physical meaning behind each component.

---

## 🛠 Prerequisites and the Role of Calculus

Physics III uses Calculus as a practical modeling tool. We will not pursue pure mathematical derivations from scratch, but rather the ability to translate a physical problem into familiar mathematical operations:

* **Calculus Tools (I, II, and III):** Understanding the concept of rates of change (derivatives) and the accumulation/summation of infinitesimal pieces (integrals). Knowing how to apply basic integration techniques to solve the modeled problems.
* **Geometry and Vectors:** Vector decomposition, dot product (crucial for flux and work), and cross product (essential in magnetism). A basic understanding of how cylindrical and spherical coordinates simplify our lives when dealing with spatial symmetries.
* **Mechanics Concepts (Physics I):** Conservation of energy, work done by a force, and Newton's Laws applied to the motion of particles and charges.

> [!NOTE]
> 
> Having a solid grasp of Calculus I and II helps immensely when starting this module. However, Multivariable Calculus (Calculus III) is what unlocks a true conceptual understanding rather than just formula memorization. Therefore, the ideal approach is to study this module alongside Calculus III or after having mastered it, allowing you to truly comprehend line, surface, and volume integrals applied to physical fields.

## 🗺 Learning Flow (Roadmap)

The content progresses linearly from isolated static phenomena to the full unification of dynamic fields and electromagnetic waves:

### ⚡ Block 1: Electrostatics (Static Electric Fields)

* **Electric Charge and Coulomb's Law:** The fundamental behavior of charges (quantization and conservation) and the vector force between point charges.
* **Electric Field and Continuous Distributions:** The concept of a field as a spatial disturbance. How to slice a charged object (rod, ring, disk) into infinitesimal pieces $dq$ and use integrals to sum the total field.
* **Electric Flux and Gauss's Law:** The geometric interpretation of field lines crossing closed surfaces. Using Gaussian surfaces to find the electric field of symmetrical shapes in a highly efficient way. **[1st Maxwell Equation]**

### 🔋 Block 2: Potential, Energy, and Dieléctrics

* **Electric Potential:** The concept of voltage ($V$) associated with work and the system's potential energy. The mathematical relationship between the electric field gradient and potential difference.
* **Capacitance and Dielectrics:** How to store energy directly in electric fields using capacitors, and how insulating materials (dielectrics) alter field behavior microscopically.

### 🧲 Block 3: Magnetostatics (Static Magnetic Fields)

* **The Magnetic Field ($\vec{B}$) and Gauss's Law for Magnetism:** The origin of magnetism, the physical meaning behind the absence of magnetic monopoles, and how magnetic fields deflect moving charges (Lorentz Force). **[2nd Maxwell Equation]**
* **Sources of Magnetic Field (Biot-Savart & Ampère's Law):** How moving charges and steady currents generate magnetic fields. Using the Biot-Savart Law to integrate differential current elements ($Id\vec{s}$) and Ampère's Law as a symmetry tool for fields. **[Base of the 4th Maxwell Equation]**

### 🌀 Block 4: Dynamic Electromagnetism and Maxwell's Equations

* **Faraday's Law and Electromagnetic Induction:** The foundation of electrical machines. How a time-varying magnetic field induces an electromotive force (EMF) and creates an induced electric field (incorporating Lenz's Law for energy conservation). **[3rd Maxwell Equation]**
* **The Displacement Current and Maxwell's Equations:** Understanding the asymmetry that Maxwell resolved by introducing the displacement current in capacitors. Synthesizing the entire physics core into Maxwell's four definitive equations. **[4th Maxwell Equation Complete]**
* **Electromagnetic Waves:** The ultimate conclusion of the course: light and radio waves viewed as self-propagating electromagnetic disturbances traveling through space, carrying energy and momentum.
