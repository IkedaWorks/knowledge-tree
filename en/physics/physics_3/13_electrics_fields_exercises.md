## 🟢 Section 1: Foundations and Kinematics (Easy Level)

### Problem 1.1 (Kinematics and Equilibrium of Multiple Charges)

A point charge $q_1 = +4.0\ \mu\text{C}$ is fixed at the spatial origin $(0,0,0)$ in a vacuum. A second point charge $q_2 = -9.0\ \mu\text{C}$ is fixed at the position $\vec{r}_2 = 4\hat{i}\ (\text{m})$. A small non-conducting ball carrying a generic charge $Q$ is placed at a point $P$ along the $x$-axis.

- **a)** Analytically determine the exact coordinate $x_P$ where the ball can be placed such that the net electric field generated by $q_1$ and $q_2$ is strictly the null vector $\vec{0}$.
    
- **b)** If the ball has a mass $m = 2.0\text{ g}$ and a charge $Q = +1.0\ \mu\text{C}$, and is released from rest at $x = 1.0\text{ m}$, calculate its instantaneous initial acceleration vector $\vec{a}$ using Newton's Second Law.
    

## 🟡 Section 2: Symmetry and Contour Integration (Medium Level)

### Problem 2.1 (Modified Halliday - Finite Charged Rod with Linear Deflection)

A thin, non-conducting rod of finite length $L$ is placed along the $x$-axis, extending from $x = -L/2$ to $x = +L/2$. A total net charge $Q$ is uniformly distributed along its length with a constant linear charge density $\lambda$. A point $P$ is located on the positive $y$-axis at a structural distance $\vec{r}_P = y\hat{j}$.

- **a)** Set up the differential charge element $dq$ and write the relative position vector $\vec{r}'$ that points from an arbitrary element $dx$ on the rod to the target point $P$.
    
- **b)** Use continuous integration to find the exact vector expression for the electric field $\vec{E}(y)$ at point $P$.
    
- **c)** Show that when the distance becomes macroscopic ($y \gg L$), the complex integral expression asymptotically collapses back into the standard Coulomb equation for point charges:
    
    $$\vec{E} \approx \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{y^2}\hat{j}$$
    

### Problem 2.2 (Modified Sadiku - Charged Disks and Surface Densities)

A flat circular disk of radius $R$ is positioned horizontally in the $xy$-plane, centered at the origin. It carries a uniform surface charge density $\sigma_0$.

- **a)** Slice the disk into concentric rings of radius $r$ and thickness $dr$ to set up the integral for the total electric field $\vec{E}(z)$ at a height $z$ along the axis of symmetry (the $z$-axis).
    
- **b)** Evaluate the integral to determine the electric field vector $\vec{E}(z)$.
    
- **c)** If the radius $R \to \infty$ (representing an infinite sheet of charge), deduce the resulting electric field vector and explain why its magnitude becomes completely invariant (independent) with respect to the distance $z$.
    

## 🔴 Section 3: Advanced Analytical Engineering (Hard Level)

### Problem 3.1 (JEE Advanced - Semi-Infinite Non-Uniform Dipole)

A thin non-conducting semi-infinite rod is placed along the positive $x$-axis, from $x = d$ to $x \to \infty$. The rod has a non-uniform linear charge density given by $\lambda(x) = \lambda_0 \frac{d}{x}$, where $\lambda_0$ is a positive constant. An unknown point charge $q$ is placed at the origin $(0,0,0)$.

- **a)** Set up the differential vector field element $d\vec{E}$ at the origin due to an infinitesimal segment $dx$ of the rod.
    
- **b)** Calculate the total electric field vector $\vec{E}_{\text{rod}}$ at the origin by evaluating the continuous integration.
    
- **c)** Determine the value of the point charge $q$ (in terms of $\lambda_0$ and $d$) such that a positive test charge placed at $x = \frac{d}{2}$ experiences an absolute net force equal to $\vec{0}$.
    

### Problem 3.2 (ITA - Relativistic Limit and Simple Harmonic Oscillations)

Two identical positive point charges $+Q$ are fixed on the $y$-axis at coordinates $(0, a, 0)$ and $(0, -a, 0)$. A third point charge $-q$ (with mass $m$) is constrained to move exclusively along the $x$-axis. It is released from rest at a generic coordinate $(x, 0, 0)$.

- **a)** Derive the exact vector expression for the total electric field $\vec{E}(x)$ generated by the two positive charges at any point on the $x$-axis.
    
- **b)** Assuming the microscopic structural constraint $x \ll a$, apply the binomial expansion to prove that the electric field behaves linearly with distance near the origin.
    
- **c)** Show that for small displacements ($x \ll a$), the target charge $-q$ executes Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) and derive the algebraic expression for its angular frequency $\omega$.
    

## 🔬 Section 4: Solutions Manual

### Solution 1.1 (Easy)

**a) Finding the Zero-Field Point:** Since $q_1$ and $q_2$ have opposite signs, the null point cannot lie between them. Because $|q_1| < |q_2|$, the null point must lie to the left of $q_1$, meaning $x_P < 0$. Let $d = |x_P|$.

$$E_{\text{net}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{|q_1|}{d^2} - \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{|q_2|}{(4 + d)^2} = 0 \implies \frac{4}{d^2} = \frac{9}{(4 + d)^2}$$

Extracting the square root of both sides:

$$\frac{2}{d} = \frac{3}{4 + d} \implies 8 + 2d = 3d \implies d = 8\text{ m}$$

Since the point is to the left of the origin, the spatial coordinate is **$x_P = -8.0\text{ m}$**.

**b) Initial Acceleration Vector:** At $x = 1.0\text{ m}$, the distance to $q_1$ is $1\text{ m}$ (repulsion $\to +\hat{i}$) and the distance to $q_2$ is $3\text{ m}$ (attraction $\to +\hat{i}$).

$$E_{\text{net}} = (8.99 \times 10^9) \left[ \frac{4.0 \times 10^{-6}}{1^2} + \frac{9.0 \times 10^{-6}}{3^2} \right] = 44950\text{ N/C}$$

$$\vec{F} = Q\vec{E} = (1.0 \times 10^{-6}\text{ C})(44950\hat{i}\text{ N/C}) = 0.04495\hat{i}\text{ N}$$

$$\vec{a} = \frac{\vec{F}}{m} = \frac{0.04495\hat{i}\text{ N}}{2.0 \times 10^{-3}\text{ kg}} = \mathbf{22.48\hat{i}\ \text{m/s}^2}$$

### Solution 2.1 (Medium)

**a) Setup:** An element of the rod at position $x$ has charge $dq = \lambda dx = (Q/L)dx$. The relative position vector from $dx$ to $P(0,y)$ is $\vec{r}' = -x\hat{i} + y\hat{j}$. The distance magnitude is $r' = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$.

**b) Integration:**

$$d\vec{E} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{dq}{(r')^3}\vec{r}' = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{\lambda dx}{(x^2 + y^2)^{3/2}} (-x\hat{i} + y\hat{j})$$

By geometric symmetry, the $\hat{i}$ components cancel perfectly over the symmetric integration limits from $-L/2$ to $+L/2$. We integrate only the $\hat{j}$ component:

$$E_y = \frac{\lambda y}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \int_{-L/2}^{L/2} \frac{1}{(x^2 + y^2)^{3/2}} \, dx$$

Using the standard trigonometric substitution integral from calculus ($x = y\tan\theta$):

$$E_y = \frac{\lambda y}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \left[ \frac{x}{y^2\sqrt{x^2+y^2}} \right]_{-L/2}^{L/2} = \frac{\lambda y}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \left( \frac{L}{y^2\sqrt{(L/2)^2 + y^2}} \right)$$

Since $\lambda L = Q$, the final vector field expression is:

$$\vec{E}(y) = \mathbf{\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0 y \sqrt{\frac{L^2}{4} + y^2}} \hat{j}}$$

**c) Asymptotic Limit ($y \gg L$):** If $y \gg L$, then the term $L^2/4$ inside the square root becomes completely negligible compared to $y^2$. Thus, $\sqrt{L^2/4 + y^2} \approx \sqrt{y^2} = y$. Substituting this back yields:

$$\vec{E} \approx \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{y^2}\hat{j}$$

### Solution 2.2 (Medium)

**a) Setup:** A ring of radius $r$ and width $dr$ has an area $dA = 2\pi r dr$ and charge $dq = \sigma_0 (2\pi r dr)$. The distance to a point $z$ on the axis is $\sqrt{r^2+z^2}$. The horizontal components cancel, leaving only the axial component multiplied by $\cos\theta = \frac{z}{\sqrt{r^2+z^2}}$.

$$dE_z = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{\sigma_0 (2\pi r dr)}{r^2 + z^2} \left( \frac{z}{\sqrt{r^2+z^2}} \right) = \frac{\sigma_0 z}{4\epsilon_0} \frac{2r\,dr}{(r^2 + z^2)^{3/2}}$$

**b) Evaluation:**

$$\vec{E}(z) = \frac{\sigma_0 z}{4\epsilon_0} \int_{0}^{R} (r^2 + z^2)^{-3/2} (2r\,dr) \hat{k} = \frac{\sigma_0 z}{4\epsilon_0} \left[ \frac{-2}{\sqrt{r^2 + z^2}} \right]_{0}^{R} \hat{k}$$

$$\vec{E}(z) = \mathbf{\frac{\sigma_0}{2\epsilon_0} \left( 1 - \frac{z}{\sqrt{R^2 + z^2}} \right) \hat{k}}$$

**c) Infinite Limit ($R \to \infty$):** As $R \to \infty$, the fraction $\frac{z}{\sqrt{R^2 + z^2}} \to 0$. The expression collapses to:

$$\vec{E} = \frac{\sigma_0}{2\epsilon_0}\hat{k}$$

The distance parameter $z$ vanishes completely because the infinite field lines run perfectly parallel to each other, maintaining a constant flux concentration through space.

### Solution 3.1 (Hard)

**a) Differential Field Vector:**

$$dq = \lambda(x)dx = \lambda_0 \frac{d}{x}dx \implies d\vec{E} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \left( \frac{\lambda_0 d \cdot dx}{x^3} \right) \hat{i}$$

**b) Evaluation via Improper Integral:**

$$\vec{E}_{\text{rod}} = -\frac{\lambda_0 d}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \left( \int_{d}^{\infty} x^{-3} \, dx \right) \hat{i} = -\frac{\lambda_0 d}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \left[ \frac{x^{-2}}{-2} \right]_{d}^{\infty} \hat{i} = \mathbf{- \frac{\lambda_0}{8\pi\epsilon_0 d} \hat{i}}$$

**c) Equilibrium at $x = d/2$:** The field of the point charge $q$ (at the origin) acting on position $d/2$ is $\vec{E}_q = \frac{4q}{4\pi\epsilon_0 d^2}\hat{i}$. The field of the rod evaluated at position $d/2$ requires shifting the distance variable to $(x - d/2)$:

$$\vec{E}_{\text{rod}}(d/2) = -\frac{\lambda_0 d}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \int_{d}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x(x - d/2)^2} \, dx \, \hat{i}$$

Solving via partial fraction decomposition:

$$\vec{E}_{\text{rod}}(d/2) = -\frac{\lambda_0}{4\pi\epsilon_0 d} \left[ 4\ln(2) - 2 \right]\hat{i}$$

Setting $\vec{E}_q + \vec{E}_{\text{rod}}(d/2) = \vec{0}$ yields the absolute algebraic solution for the charge:

$$\mathbf{q = \frac{\lambda_0 d}{4} \left[ 2 \ln(2) - 1 \right]}$$

### Solution 3.2 (Hard)

**a) Vector Expression:**

$$\vec{E}(x) = 2 \cdot \left( \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{Q}{x^2 + a^2} \right) \cos\theta \, \hat{i} = \mathbf{\frac{2Qx}{4\pi\epsilon_0 (x^2 + a^2)^{3/2}} \hat{i}}$$

**b) Binomial Approximation ($x \ll a$):**

$$\vec{E}(x) = \frac{2Qx}{4\pi\epsilon_0 a^3} \left(1 + \frac{x^2}{a^2}\right)^{-3/2} \approx \frac{2Qx}{4\pi\epsilon_0 a^3} \left( 1 - \frac{3}{2}\frac{x^2}{a^2} \dots \right)$$

Since $x \ll a$, the higher-order terms collapse ($x^3/a^5 \to 0$):

$$\vec{E}(x) \approx \mathbf{\frac{Q}{2\pi\epsilon_0 a^3} x \, \hat{i}}$$

**c) SHM Framework:** The dynamic restoring force on $-q$ is $\vec{F} = -q\vec{E}(x) = - \left( \frac{qQ}{2\pi\epsilon_0 a^3} \right) x \, \hat{i}$. This strictly obeys Hooke's Law format $\vec{F} = -k_{\text{eff}}x$, confirming simple harmonic motion.

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k_{\text{eff}}}{m}} = \mathbf{\sqrt{\frac{qQ}{2\pi\epsilon_0 m a^3}}}$$
